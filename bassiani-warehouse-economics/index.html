Anatomy of a Warehouse Rave: What 14 Hours at Bassiani in Tbilisi Actually Costs to Run

By Noor Halabi
Bassiani, the Georgian warehouse club tucked under a stadium in central Tbilisi, has spent six years being held up as the model for a working European underground club. It is also, by its own resident bookers' admission, a very thin business. We spent a Saturday in the basement with the operations team and one with a calculator, and here is what one full 14-hour cycle actually costs.

Photo: Unsplash / Pexels

The numbers are in euros, rounded to the nearest 50, and cover one Friday-Saturday programming slot from 23:30 doors to 13:30 close. Everything is a real line item from the cycle running on 18 April 2026.

Door, headliners, residents

Doors brought in 41,800 EUR. Capacity is 2400 and the night sold 2030 tickets at an average paid price of 22.50 EUR after early-bird tiers and student concessions. Of that, 4,180 EUR went to ticketing fees and platform cut.

The international headliner cost 6,500 EUR plus 1,100 EUR in flights and accommodation. The two warmup residents took 600 EUR each. A closing b2b between two locals took 1,400 EUR shared. Total artist line: 10,200 EUR.

Sound, staff, security

The in-house Funktion-One rig is owned, so there is no rental, but maintenance and the touring engineer for the night cost 850 EUR. Lighting (a fixed install plus one extra haze tech) ran 420 EUR. Security was 18 staff at 110 EUR each plus a head-of-door at 280 EUR, total 2,260 EUR. Bar and floor staff (22 people across three rotations) came in at 2,950 EUR.

The boring expensive stuff

Power: 1,400 EUR for the night. Cleaning crew, including the brutal Sunday-morning four-hour deep clean, 980 EUR. Insurance amortised per event, 600 EUR. Licensing and PRO/PPL royalty payments based on door, 1,200 EUR. Door medic and harm-reduction table staff, 540 EUR.

The bar saves the business

Bar revenue net of cost of goods was 19,400 EUR. Without the bar, the night would have lost about 14,000 EUR before any rent or admin allocation. With the bar, the night cleared roughly 16,000 EUR before rent.

The space's rent allocation per night was 11,500 EUR. The night, all in, cleared 4,500 EUR profit on 61,200 EUR of revenue. A 7.4% margin on what looked from the outside like a sold-out international rave.

What this means for the scene

This is why ticketed afterhours in Tbilisi keep getting more expensive: not greed, just thin math. A single security overrun, a missed flight, or a quiet Friday and that 7% margin disappears. The bookers we spoke to are upfront about it. "We do not run this place to make money," one said. "We run it to keep being able to run it."

"Bassiani opened the books for one Friday-Saturday cycle. The numbers behind a 2400-cap warehouse rave are less glamorous, and less profitable, than the scene mythology suggests."
The romanticism around the underground club tends to skip the spreadsheet. Bassiani's spreadsheet is the same as fabric's, as Tresor's, as Bossa Nova in NYC's. Door is not the business. The bar is the business. And the artist-line is the smallest negotiable item, which is why the same six headliners cycle through the same fifteen rooms every season. The math does not allow a wider field.
