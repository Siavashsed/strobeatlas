Halloween Is Now a Genre: Mexico City, NYC, and Berlin Built Three Different October 31 Subscenes

By Riv Solano
For most of the last decade, halloween in club terms meant one of two things: a costume night that closed on October 31 regardless of which weekday it fell on, or a chaotic Friday-or-Saturday-nearest-to-31 catch-all with smeared face paint and a confused door policy. By 2025, three cities had broken away. Mexico City, New York, and Berlin now each program a full halloween week with its own internal logic. We were on the floor in all three of them last year. The differences are sharper than the marketing copy suggests.

Photo: Unsplash / Pexels

What follows is a side-by-side: same week, same calendar dates, three radically different programming philosophies. The data points are from venues that ran in every city and shared their numbers with us under condition of light anonymisation.

Mexico City: the rooftop run

CDMX's halloween subscene is a five-night rooftop run from October 28 to November 1, with nights spread across Roma Norte, Condesa, and the Centro warehouse district. The dominant sound is hard groove and tribal techno, with a long-running tradition of one Indira Paganotto-or-equivalent headliner per night. Door is roughly 600 to 1800 MXN. Sound systems are loud-but-uneven, rooftop-deployed Funktion-One or Void towers in compromised acoustic environments.

The night we saw at Sala Bizarro's rooftop on 31 October 2025 ran 22:00 to 06:00, sold 1820 of 1800 paid capacity, had no costume gate at the door, and crucially the city's municipal noise regulator allowed an extension to 06:00 across the entire Roma Norte block as part of a Dia de Muertos cultural exception. That regulatory carve-out is the reason CDMX is winning the halloween booking race in the Americas.

NYC: the basement run

NYC's halloween is denser and more compressed: typically just October 30 to November 1, three nights, almost entirely below ground. Brooklyn dominates with 3 Dollar Bill, Public Records, and Nowadays running coordinated bookings that swap headliners across venues. Sound is generally more pristine (these are fixed installs, not deploys), and the lineups lean drum-and-bass-heavy this year - a noticeable shift from the techno-dominant programming of 2022 to 2024.

Door is steeper (40 to 65 USD), capacities are smaller (400 to 900 paid per night per venue), and the door policy at the better venues is now strictly enforced costume-required, which the bookers say has improved crowd quality (and the bar tab) materially.

Berlin: the slow build

Berlin treats halloween the way Berlin treats every commercial holiday: with mild suspicion and a refusal to programme around it. The subscene that has emerged is, characteristically, an anti-subscene. Three or four select rooms (Tresor, RSO, Sisyphos for the seasonal closer) run dedicated halloween nights, but they are intentionally low-key, no costume gate, low-on-the-marketing, headliner choices that are almost demonstratively unrelated to anything October-coded. Door prices barely move. Capacities sell out anyway.

The interesting thing about Berlin halloween is that it has barely grown in three years while CDMX has roughly tripled and NYC has roughly doubled. The city's existing infrastructure (24-hour licensing, established weekend programming, residents who can fill rooms without seasonal hooks) makes the holiday genuinely unnecessary. It is the only one of the three cities where halloween is not a meaningful programming event for most clubs.

What this tells us about the format

Halloween used to be an excuse. It is now an annual production cycle with its own sound profiles, regulatory tailwinds (CDMX), regulatory neutrality (NYC), and regulatory irrelevance (Berlin). The subscenes that are growing are the ones in cities where halloween offers something the regular week cannot: extended hours, a costume-defined room, a one-night-only booking pull. Berlin's regular week already offers all of those things, so halloween there will probably stay flat. The two North American scenes are still building.

"Three cities have turned halloween from one chaotic night into a programmed week with its own sound, its own bookings, and its own economics. The differences between them say more than any of them would on their own."
If you are picking one halloween night for 2026, the smart money is on the second Friday of CDMX's run (typically October 30 this year), at a Roma Norte rooftop, with Indira Paganotto or one of her circle on the lineup. If you want pristine sound and a denser room, the Brooklyn basement scene on the Saturday is the better play. Berlin is for people who would have gone out that weekend anyway.
